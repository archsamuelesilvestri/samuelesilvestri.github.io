Renovating a home in Italy from abroad

25 November 2026, by Samuele Silvestri, architect

Buying a home in Italy is often the start of a renovation. If you live abroad, the questions are usually the same: which permits you need, what you can change, and how to follow the works without being on site.

Permits

Every renovation in Italy needs the right building permit for the works involved. Repainting or replacing floors usually needs none. Moving internal walls or renewing the systems usually requires a CILA, filed with the municipality by an architect or engineer. Work on the structure requires a SCIA, while new buildings and major transformations need a building permit (permesso di costruire). When the layout changes, the land registry (catasto) must also be updated at the end of the works.

Historic centres and listed buildings

Many of the homes that attract buyers from abroad are in historic centres. There, municipal rules decide what can be changed, and listed buildings also need the approval of the Soprintendenza, the heritage authority. It is worth checking before buying, because it affects both what you can do and how long it takes.

An experience in Rome

In 2022, working with Interalia Architects in Rome, I followed the renovation of an apartment on Piazza Navona for an English client, in the heart of the city's historic centre.

Following the works from abroad

I work in English, and a project can be followed remotely. Renders, virtual reality and videos, made with VISION, show the spaces before they are built; video calls replace most meetings, and your visits to Italy can be planned for the key moments, such as the main design choices and the end of the works. The studio handles the building permits and supervises the site.
